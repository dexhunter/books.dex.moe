投行职业进阶指南by王大力A Career Guide to Investment Banking by Wang Dali

因为我一直有关注王大力的知乎和公众号，最近正好看到了他发了一本新书《投行职业进阶指南》，觉得蛮有意思的便在淘宝上下单。在淘宝搜索的时候发现书的定价是59，然后处于预售阶段，最便宜一家只要14元，但拍了之后店家立马告诉我没货让我退款，我觉得肯定是卖家看自己卖的太低了就不愿意退款，但也不能申诉，因为他的预售期要到8月31日，而我又不想等那么久，所以就在其他店里下单（35元）买了这本书。这本书的完整标题是《投行职业进阶指南：从新手到合伙人》，虽然这本书很明显的目标人群是还没进入或者刚进入投行工作的年轻人，而我其实跟投行没特别大的关系，但是我还是买回来（被割了一波韭菜）读了读。首先我还是需要吐槽下这本书有很多套话废话，感觉的确很多没什么营养的建议，比如说你的简历要简洁明了，名校的重要性，没有名校的话就看实习经历和考取有用的证书等等。这些都没问题，但是作为一本介绍投行的书来说过于general（泛大众化），因为这个放到任何一个领域都是适用的，而不仅仅是投行。我觉得这本书最大的问题是我没有从中获取我之前不知道的信息，所有信息都是符合我的常识认知的，而我也不需要别人来reinforce（强化）我的常识认知，这造成了我觉得这本书非常平庸的观点，即使这是一本畅销书。

虽然定了这本书很一般的基调，但我还是认真看完了这本书并有所思考。首先我没有被这本打鸡血让我觉得自己需要立马努力学习或者工作，虽然从书中内容来说，投行实习生很多工作都非常非常基础，比如记录会议内容，勘察错别字之类的，但是不能否认的一点是投行离钱很近，所以收入基本没有上限。

I’ve been following Wang Dali’s Zhihu and WeChat official account for a while, and recently I happened to see he’d released a new book, A Career Guide to Investment Banking. It seemed pretty interesting, so I ordered it on Taobao. When I searched on Taobao, I found the book’s list price was 59 yuan and it was in pre-sale, with the cheapest shop selling it for only 14 yuan. But right after I placed the order, the seller told me it was out of stock and asked me to request a refund. I’m sure the seller just thought they’d priced it too low and didn’t want to go through with it, but I couldn’t file a complaint either, since their pre-sale period ran until August 31, and I didn’t want to wait that long. So I ordered the book from another shop (35 yuan). The book’s full title is A Career Guide to Investment Banking: From Rookie to Partner. Although the target audience is obviously young people who haven’t yet entered or have just started working in investment banking, and I really don’t have much to do with investment banking, I still bought it (and got fleeced like a leek) and read it. First, I have to complain that the book has a lot of boilerplatefiller; it really feels like a lot of the advice has no substance, like keeping your resume concise and clear, the importance of a top school, and if you don’t have one, then internship experience and earning useful certificates matter, and so on. None of that is wrong, but for a book introducing investment banking it’s too general, since it applies to any field, not just investment banking. I think the book’s biggest problem is that I didn’t get any information from it that I didn’t already know. Everything fit my common-sense understanding, and I don’t need anyone to reinforce my common sense. That’s what makes me see the book as very mediocre, even though it’s a bestseller.

Although I’ve set the tone that this book is pretty mediocre, I still read it carefully all the way through and did some thinking. First, it didn’t fire me up into feeling I needed to start studying or working hard right away. Although, judging from the book, a lot of what investment banking interns do is very, very basic, like taking meeting notes and checking for typos, one thing you can’t deny is that investment banking is close to the money, so the income basically has no ceiling.
