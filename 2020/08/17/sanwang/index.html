棋王树王孩子王by阿城The King of Chess, The King of Trees, The King of Children by Ah Cheng

棋王讲的是一个痴迷于象棋的人的故事。
树王讲的是一个护林人的故事。
孩子王讲的是一个教书人的故事。

故事细节我就不赘述了，都不长，感兴趣的可以自行阅读。我读完后觉得有些感动，书中的人物非常生动形象，像是我们身边的人但又是有时代特色/背景的。作家创作肯定收到时局的影响，但人性是不变的。人性中的善良良知在文革的时代背景下显得更加难能可贵。棋王的故事总让我想起匠人精神，我自己最近也在当木匠和油漆工，自己定制电吉他，但做了三周最后效果跟自己买到的成品区别还是很大，或者网上的视频的结果跟我的也很有区别。现在我觉得自己还是不浪费时间和精力在木匠和油漆上，可以画更少的时间用来拼装或者制作电路。我看我本科学校工业设计和建筑有买各种木匠设备（cnc，打磨机，喷漆仪器等等）感叹自己如果选了那些专业说不定就可以在学校做吉他了（降低成本+提高工艺）。不过像棋王中提到的精神更接近于一直废寝忘食的痴迷，我原本以为自己也会有这样的感受，但现实中我发现我并没有，工作就是工作，生活更多是吃喝拉撒睡组成的。我并没有一直痴迷于某样事物，特别是互联网的时代我感觉自己的注意力很分散，很容易被不同的事物所吸引。

树王中让我印象最深的是树王的发问：什么是有用的树？什么是没用的树？的确，就如同我定制吉他时我爸我问我“你做这个有什么意义”一样，我觉得很多事情的意义是人赋予的，或者是被时代赋予的，比如现在孩子如果做的事情不能赚钱的话那么家长就会觉得“没意义”，又比如读书，会让你思考，但并不能直接让你赚钱，我觉得其实他们是忽略了这样没意义的事情丰富了我们的人生。我做了很多没用的事情，有时候是为了自己的愉悦，有时候我自己也觉得没什么意义，但我就是想把这个做好（指上油漆时我反复的上漆又去漆的过程）。我们的价值观的确由时代所主导，但很难说当今时代的价值观就是“正确的”，可能时对于时代来说最有用的，比如金钱至上。但我总觉得我们应该也包容不同的价值观，允许不同的声音出现，虽然说中国主流声音会声称“不畏惧辩论”，但实际上他们只允许符合自己价值观的嘉宾进行讨论。我之前看过传销的条件第一条就是“1、一个封闭的环境，传销人员基本上不会让人跑出去或者听外部的消息。听也只是让你听对传销人员有利的消息。换句话就是你进入传销窝你出不去，而你所有听到的消息都是传销人员给你的。” 所以我觉得媒体应该是自由和独立的，即使有监管，但应该也要让不同声音发声，人们自己是有理智判断正误的，不能把别人当作傻子。

孩子王的故事让我最为感动，虽然故事主线是围绕教书先生和学生的故事，但讲了一个有能力的人被欺负受到不公正待遇的事情，老师只是想教一些对学生真正有用的事情不想拘于形式，结果就被地方领导辞退了。王福最后写的作业让人潸然泪下，就是这个学生不仅仅很聪明，也善于细心观察。

我后来又买了阿城的新作《河图洛书》和《昙曜五窟》，书上的插图十分精美。有机会也会写写我的感悟。

The King of Chess is about a man obsessed with Chinese chess.
The King of Trees is about a forest keeper.
The King of Children is about a teacher.

I won’t go over the details of the stories. None of them are long, so if you’re interested you can read them yourself. After finishing them I felt pretty moved. The characters are vivid and lifelike, like people around us, but also very much marked by their era and background. A writer’s work is surely shaped by the times, but human nature doesn’t change. The kindness and conscience in human nature feel all the more precious against the backdrop of the Cultural Revolution. The King of Chess always reminds me of craftsmanship. Lately I’ve been playing carpenter and painter myself, building a custom electric guitar, but after three weeks the result is still a long way off from the finished ones I could buy, or from what people get in online videos. Now I think I shouldn’t waste time and energy on the carpentry and painting, and could spend less time on it and more on assembly or building circuits. I saw that the industrial design and architecture departments at my undergrad school had bought all kinds of woodworking equipment (CNC, sanders, spray-painting rigs, etc.), and I can’t help thinking that if I’d picked one of those majors I might have been able to build guitars at school (lower cost + better craftsmanship). But the spirit in The King of Chess is closer to a constant obsession where you forget to eat and sleep. I originally thought I’d feel that way about something too, but in reality I found that I don’t. Work is just work, and life is mostly eating, drinking, going to the bathroom, and sleeping. I’ve never stayed obsessed with any one thing, and especially in the internet age I feel my attention is scattered and I’m easily drawn to all sorts of different things.

What struck me most in The King of Trees is the question the King of Trees asks: what is a useful tree? What is a useless tree? It’s true. Just like when I was building my custom guitar and my dad asked me “what’s the point of doing this,” I think the meaning of a lot of things is given by people, or given by the times. For example, nowadays if what a kid does can’t make money, parents think it’s “pointless.” Or take reading: it makes you think, but it doesn’t directly make you money. I think what they overlook is that these “pointless” things enrich our lives. I’ve done a lot of useless things. Sometimes it’s for my own enjoyment, and sometimes even I don’t think there’s much point, but I just want to do it well (like the process of painting, where I kept putting on coats and stripping them off again). Our values really are dominated by our era, but it’s hard to say the values of today are the “right” ones; maybe they’re just the most useful for the era, like money above all else. But I always feel we should also make room for different values and allow different voices to be heard. Mainstream voices in China may claim they “aren’t afraid of debate,” but in practice they only let guests who share their values join the discussion. I once read about the conditions for pyramid schemes, and the first one was: “1. A closed environment. Pyramid scheme operators basically won’t let people leave or hear news from outside. Whatever you do hear is only news that benefits the operators. In other words, once you enter the pyramid scheme den you can’t get out, and all the information you hear comes from the operators.” So I think the media should be free and independent. Even with regulation, different voices should still be allowed to speak. People are capable of judging right from wrong on their own, and you can’t treat them like fools.

The King of Children moved me the most. Although the main storyline is about a teacher and his students, it tells of a capable person who gets bullied and treated unfairly. The teacher just wants to teach the students things that are actually useful to them instead of sticking to formalities, and as a result he’s dismissed by the local leaders. The essay Wang Fu writes at the end brings tears to your eyes; this student is not only very smart but also great at careful observation.

Later I also bought Ah Cheng’s newer books, Hetu Luoshu and The Five Caves of Tanyao, and the illustrations in them are beautiful. When I get the chance I’ll write about my thoughts on those too.
