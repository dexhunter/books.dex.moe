### Where to start reading?

In the very first class, the professor showed us that the preface of the Records is a combination of an introductory chapter and a table of contents, which is why it's in the last chapter of the whole book, the Grand Historian's Own Preface, telling the story of Sima Tan and Sima Qian. It's the story of how Sima Tan raised Sima Qian and turned him into an outstanding Grand Historian: not only teaching by word and example and hiring the best teachers, but also funding Sima Qian's travels north and south of the Yangtze, walking ten thousand li. When Emperor Wu of Han performed the Feng and Shan sacrifices at Mount Tai, he didn't invite the Grand Historian Sima Tan to go with him. Sima Tan took this as the will of Heaven and fell bedridden, but he still saw it as his duty to write a work that would be passed down across the land, and handed that mission on to Sima Qian.
1. Filial piety: serving your parents - serving your ruler - establishing yourself (Classic of Filial Piety): "Establish yourself and walk the Way, make your name known to later generations so as to bring glory to your parents; this is the culmination of filial piety."
2. The sense of responsibility of Chinese scholars and men of purpose
3. The Book of Songs, the Book of Documents, the Rites, Music, and the Spring and Autumn Annals
4. "When it comes to benevolence, do not yield even to your teacher" - a duty you cannot shirk
5. "Aspiration is the heart of a scholar."
6. (Zuo Zhuan) Establish virtue, establish deeds, establish words

### The Grand Historian's Own Preface

1. Twelve "Basic Annals" -> recording the center of the world, those able to rule over all under Heaven
2. Ten "Tables" -> laying out the general trends of the world and the broad outline of order, chaos, rise and fall, so as to see the gains and losses of each era
3. Eight "Treatises"
4. "Hereditary Houses"
5. "Biographies"
6. 130 chapters in total, 526,500 characters, originally titled "The Grand Historian"; designed with a master copy and a duplicate copy, and passed down for over 2,000 years

### The qualities of the Records

1. History - the ancestor of the official histories
2. Collected works - a pillar of prose literature
3. Masters - a school of thought of its own
4. Classic - the great law for a hundred kings

"Not merely a fine history of one era, but clearly the great law for a hundred kings."


### Why the Records are great

1. Hard work, across two generations, father and son
2. Genius: "free-flowing and unconventional, with a remarkable spirit"
3. Source material: "lost writings and ancient events, all gathered without exception"
4. Family heritage: a family of astronomers, a family of Grand Historians
5. Education: Kong Anguo, Dong Zhongshu
6. Experience: at twenty he set out on a grand tour of the realm
7. **A higher state of mind**, "the heavenly spring" (Zhuangzi said: "Those whose desires run deep have shallow heavenly springs") -> the Grand Historian's state of mind came from loyalty and filial piety
8. "Doing your utmost is called loyalty" (doing your own job well); "bringing glory to your parents is filial piety" (making something of yourself, so others see that your parents raised you well, is the highest form of filial piety)
