### The Biography of Boyi (If this is what is called the Way of Heaven, is it right or is it wrong?)

* What era? The end of the Shang and the beginning of the Zhou
* Why first? "In a degenerate age when all scrambled for profit, they alone pursued righteousness; they gave up a kingdom and starved to death, and all under Heaven praised them. Thus I made the Biography of Boyi the first."
* "Replacing violence with violence, and not knowing it is wrong"
* The ancients' sense of righteousness had levels
* Boyi did not resent, and the Grand Historian did not resent either
* "They sought benevolence and attained benevolence; what was there to resent?"
* The craft of reading/doing things:
    * Put yourself in their shoes
    * Think it through thoroughly
    * Be attentive to the finest detail
    * See through both surface and depth
* The gentleman cherishes models (exemplars, people whose character can serve as a model), the petty man cherishes favors (small kindnesses, favors)
* Han Yu, *In Praise of Boyi*: "Someone like Boyi stands alone and acts on his own, and would not look back though it went against all of heaven and earth for ten thousand generations. His faith in the Way was firm and he knew himself clearly."
* Those who love fame will surely resort to falsehood
* "Had Heaven not given birth to Confucius, all ages would be like a long night"
* Does the Way of Heaven repay?
    * First level: seeking benevolence and attaining benevolence
    * Second level: in terms of whom, there is repayment to oneself and repayment to one's descendants; in terms of time, there is repayment in life and repayment after death; in terms of form, there is the repayment of fortune and the repayment of fame;
    * Third level: the Way of Heaven does repay, and within that repayment there is the human part; do all that is humanly possible. Only when human effort is exhausted does the Way of Heaven reveal itself. Seek only to do your utmost as a person, and do not blame Heaven.
