---
title: "A Career Guide to Investment Banking by Wang Dali"
---

I've been following Wang Dali's Zhihu and WeChat official account for a while, and recently I happened to see he'd released a new book, *A Career Guide to Investment Banking*. It seemed pretty interesting, so I ordered it on Taobao. When I searched on Taobao, I found the book's list price was 59 yuan and it was in pre-sale, with the cheapest shop selling it for only 14 yuan. But right after I placed the order, the seller told me it was out of stock and asked me to request a refund. I'm sure the seller just thought they'd priced it too low and didn't want to go through with it, but I couldn't file a complaint either, since their pre-sale period ran until August 31, and I didn't want to wait that long. So I ordered the book from another shop (35 yuan). The book's full title is *A Career Guide to Investment Banking: From Rookie to Partner*. Although the target audience is obviously young people who haven't yet entered or have just started working in investment banking, and I really don't have much to do with investment banking, I still bought it (and got fleeced like a leek) and read it. First, I have to complain that the book has a lot of <ruby>boilerplate<rt>filler</rt></ruby>; it really feels like a lot of the advice has no substance, like keeping your resume concise and clear, the importance of a top school, and if you don't have one, then internship experience and earning useful certificates matter, and so on. None of that is wrong, but for a book introducing investment banking it's too general, since it applies to any field, not just investment banking. I think the book's biggest problem is that I didn't get any information from it that I didn't already know. Everything fit my common-sense understanding, and I don't need anyone to reinforce my common sense. That's what makes me see the book as very mediocre, even though it's a bestseller.

Although I've set the tone that this book is pretty mediocre, I still read it carefully all the way through and did some thinking. First, it didn't fire me up into feeling I needed to start studying or working hard right away. Although, judging from the book, a lot of what investment banking interns do is very, very basic, like taking meeting notes and checking for typos, one thing you can't deny is that investment banking is close to the money, so the income basically has no ceiling.
